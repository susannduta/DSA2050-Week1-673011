Retail Sales Performance Analysis
Project Overview

This project analyses a retail transaction dataset to assess sales performance across regions and product categories. The analysis applies basic data science methods to transform transaction-level data into meaningful business performance indicators and visual insights.

The project follows a reproducible analytical workflow, beginning with problem framing and dataset inspection, followed by KPI calculation, visualization, interpretation, and development of a concise decision brief.

Business Objective

The primary objective is to evaluate retail sales performance and identify differences in revenue contribution across regions and product categories.

The analysis is intended to support a retail sales or business manager in understanding:

Overall revenue generated during the observed period
Total volume of products sold
Average revenue generated per transaction
Differences in revenue contribution across regions
Differences in revenue contribution across product categories

The analysis is descriptive and diagnostic in nature. It describes observed sales performance and examines differences across selected business dimensions without making causal claims.

Each row represents one retail order or transaction made by a customer.

Visual Analysis

Two visualizations are used to examine differences in revenue contribution.

1. Total Revenue by Region

A bar chart compares total revenue across the four regions represented in the dataset.

Kisumu recorded the highest revenue contribution at approximately KES 880,000, followed by Mombasa at approximately KES 800,000. Nakuru and Nairobi recorded approximately KES 780,000 and KES 760,000, respectively.

2. Total Revenue by Product Category

A bar chart compares total revenue across product categories.

Electronics, Personal Care, and Home & Office were the highest-revenue categories, each contributing approximately KES 920,000–930,000. Accessories recorded the lowest revenue contribution at approximately KES 420,000.

These findings describe differences in observed revenue contribution; they do not establish the causes of those differences.

Business Interpretation

The analysis provides an initial view of retail performance across two important dimensions: geographic region and product category.

The results indicate that revenue is not distributed equally across all regions or categories. The relatively close performance of several categories suggests that multiple product groups contribute substantially to overall revenue, while Accessories represents a comparatively smaller contribution within the observed transactions.

Similarly, the differences between regional revenue totals provide a basis for further investigation of regional sales performance.

These results can support management in identifying areas that warrant additional analysis, particularly when combined with information such as inventory levels, operating costs, customer characteristics, sales targets, and longer-term transaction data.

Limitation

The analysis is based on 300 transactions within the available dataset, providing a limited view of retail performance.

The observed differences in revenue describe patterns within the dataset but do not establish their underlying causes or long-term trends. The analysis also does not incorporate variables such as operating costs, inventory availability, customer demographics, profitability, or sales targets.

A larger dataset covering a longer period and incorporating additional operational variables would provide a stronger basis for evaluating retail performance.
